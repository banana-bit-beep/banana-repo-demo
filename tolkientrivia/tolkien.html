Now time for some Tolkien Trivia

  • "The Hobbit" was born on an exam paper:While grading school exams, Tolkien found a blank page left by a student and impulsively wrote, "In a hole in the ground there lived a hobbit." He had no idea what a hobbit was when he wrote it.
  • He was a master linguist: Tolkien spoke over a dozen languages (including Latin, Greek, Gothic, Old Norse, and Finnish). He created the languages of Middle-earth (like Elvish and Dwarvish) first, then built the world and stories around them so the languages would have a history.
  • C.S. Lewis was a close friend: Tolkien and C.S. Lewis (author of The Chronicles of Narnia) were close friends and members of "The Inklings," an informal literary group at Oxford. Tolkien actually played a major role in converting Lewis back to Christianity.
  • He served in World War I: Tolkien fought in the Battle of the Somme, where he lost several of his closest friends. His firsthand experience in the muddy, terrifying trenches heavily influenced his descriptions of the Dead Marshes and Mordor.
  • The word "Hobbit" was added to the Oxford English Dictionary: Tolkien worked on the staff of the Oxford English Dictionary in his youth (specifically on words starting with 'W'). Years later, the dictionary added "Hobbit" and "Tolkienian" to its official entries.
  • "Beren and Lúthien" is on his gravestone: Tolkien based the love story of the mortal man Beren and the immortal elf Lúthien on his relationship with his wife, Edith. The names "Beren" and "Lúthien" are engraved on their shared headstone in Oxford.
  • The Beatles wanted to make a LOTR movie: In the late 1960s, The Beatles approached director Stanley Kubrick to make a Lord of the Rings musical starring Paul McCartney as Frodo, Ringo Starr as Sam, George Harrison as Gandalf, and John Lennon as Gollum. Tolkien vetoed the idea.
  • He wrote letters as Father Christmas: Every December for over 20 years, Tolkien wrote illustrated letters to his children pretending to be Father Christmas, complete with stories about the North Pole, polar bears, and goblin wars.
  • Christopher Tolkien drew the original maps: Tolkien’s son, Christopher, drew the famous detailed map of Middle-earth printed in The Lord of the Rings. After his father's death, Christopher edited and published much of Tolkien's unfinished work, including The Silmarillion.
  • The publisher didn't think LOTR would make money: Allen & Unwin published The Lord of the Rings in three volumes between 1954 and 1955. Expecting to lose money, the publisher made Tolkien agree to forgo any royalties until the books had broken even. It went on to become one of the best-selling series in history.
